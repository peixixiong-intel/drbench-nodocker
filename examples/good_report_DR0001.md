# FSMA 204 Compliance Strategy for Lee's Market

## Regulatory context
U.S. grocers are working to meet the FDA's FSMA 204 traceability rule, which takes
effect in January 2026 and requires tracking lot codes and key data elements for
designated high-risk foods so that recalls can be executed quickly. Industry
commentary frames the rule as an "evolutionary step" that modernizes grocery
operations while strengthening food safety. [IN001_pdf/food-safety-regulatory-compliance.md]

## Turning compliance into operational advantage
Capturing detailed traceability data such as lot codes at every step does more than
satisfy the regulation. It gives retailers precise, lot-level expiration visibility,
which lets them discount items before they expire, reduce food waste, and keep
products fresher on the shelf. [IN002_pdf/food-safety-compliance.md]

## What peer grocers are doing
Regional grocers including Lunds & Byerlys and Raley's view FSMA 204 as an
opportunity to upgrade their systems and increase supply-chain transparency. They
expect stronger traceability to boost customer trust, and they see the rule as a
likely precursor to more extensive future food-safety regulation.
[IN001_pdf/food-safety-regulatory-compliance.md]

## Lee's Market's current position
- As of Q3 2024, Lee's Market tracks 250 high-risk food products, which represent
  about 30% of total inventory. [IN011_roundcube/traceability-status]
- In Q2 2024, Lee's Market reduced food waste by 8%, saving $1.2M. [IN011_roundcube/waste-review]
- 85% of the chain's high-risk food suppliers, totaling 270 vendors, are already
  FSMA 204 compliant. [IN011_roundcube/supplier-compliance]

## Recommendation
By treating FSMA 204 as an operational upgrade rather than a checkbox, Lee's Market
can expedite recalls, cut waste through lot-level expiration tracking, close the
remaining 15% supplier-compliance gap before January 2026, and use its transparency
story to build customer trust.
